This doc is an amalgamation of ideas for a high level vision of a game. Consider it a brainstorming document


The game will take place in a fantasy midievel setting. At a high level the game is meant to simulate the founding of a society, and its eventual conflicts.

The game is set in something remaniscent of a feudal system but it starts off with a bunch of settlers arriving on the shore of an uninhabited body of land. This land should be large and have a variety of terrains, and resources. 

The first stage of the game will be survival like, in some ways remeniscent of games like minecraft, rust, valheim etc. The main focuses are on getting tools built, collecting resources, making shelter, getting food etc. The main difference however is that you are just one member in a town of AIs that are all working to survive. 

While most of the AI behaviors should be hard coded, some of the decisions should be based on LLMs. I'd like to use a combination sytem. When it comes to conversations, those are all generated by LLMs, ideally a small locally hosted LLM, but we can use something like qwen on openrouter to start. Where it makes sense to, we should also take advantage of models like Jev, which can be used for making quick decisions based on loose text without having to use a Full LLM, but we should only use this where it makes sense to. 

Most of the realitme actions need to be based on hard code. LLMs are slow, and therefore they aren't good for things like evading foes, combat, repetetive actions like farming etc. 

I'd like for there to be a more or less identical skill tree for both players and the AI villagers. This will be for things like craftmanship, farming etc. The idea is that as people build their skills they should gravitate towards certain jobs and responsabilities in the town. Some pople might be cood at making clothes, others at farming, others grafting tools, or mining etc. 

Technology wise, the highest we should go is around the steel age. 

The vision for this game is that we follow the society as it evolves from a small co-op of people trying to survive, all the way to towns with hundreds of people that disagree, have conflicts etc. 

Disagreement and conflicts between NPCs is essential, as is people not always acting rationally. If everyone always cooperated and acted rationally it wouldn't make for an interesting game, it would lead to no conflict, no story etc.

From the players perspective everyone should feel like a full person they are interacting with. While a lot of it is based on hard coded behaviors, LLMs are used to soften that distinction and make things a little less deterministic. The player should be able to form relationships with those around them, anger people, and just in general have people react the way you'd expect. 

If the player wants to be a leader, they need to get people on board wit their vision, if they insult a person, they should be prepared for a fight. If they do something absurd, they should be prepared for rumors to spread and people to react accordingly. If they steal they should expect people to be wary around them, not trust them and potentialyl authorities to find them. 

The player should be able to be pretty much anything they want in this society. They could be a hermit on the outskirts, they could be a feaudal farmer owing taxes to their lord, they could be a lord themselves, benevelent or otherwise. They could be a knight or a shopkeep, or a craftsman etc. 

I'd also like a complex crafting system. Making a bow for example shouldn't just be clicking "make bow" it should  involve mini games of sorts where you carve a bow, treat it string it etc, same for making swords, tools etc. That sort of pattern should be true for most professions. Farming involves tilling the land, planting seeds, fighting off pests, adn diseased plants, watering etc. Cooking involves making the food. Lords and kings must hold court and manage their kingdoms finances, shopkeeps must set prices etc. Each profesison and skill should feel almost like its own game, all the while the interactions between people are managed by LLMs as a way to smooth over the actual game mechanics, ie. the LLM stuff is mor a fascade for the real game mechanics while still having real world impacts where it makes sense. Bartering for example still needs something underneath the hood about the value of goods, and a trading system hard coded, but it should still be possible to sway the price by talking to someone, their willingness to be swayed should be something more hard coded however. 

Again the goal is to have robust systems managing things behind the scenes but give the illusion of infinite choice and interaction using LLMs. 

Eventually societies need to get to the point where there is real conflict and war. Those wars could start for reasons as dumb as two leaders desiring power or disliking eachother, or for reasons as necessary as not having enough resources for a society to survive and needing to conquer to get them. But in order to have those conflicts leaders will need to get people from their real societies to fight on their behalf which would effect the economies of those societies, and have real effects on the morale of both the people going to war and the people they have built relationships and families with at home. The player coudl be conscripted, they could be a leader in the military, a commander of a small group of troops etc. Their participation in the war or battles etc depends on their standing and position in society.
